SCIENTIFIC & EDUCATIONAL SPECIFICATIONS

1. Event Horizon ≠ Black Hole Shadow

The event horizon is the immaterial causal boundary ($r_+ \le 2r_g$) beyond which light cannot escape. The shadow is an optical silhouette ($\sim 5.2r_g$) produced by photon capture, appearing 2.6× larger than the horizon coordinate radius.

2. Kerr Metric & Asymmetry

Rotating spacetime drags light paths in the direction of spin. Prograde photons orbit significantly closer than retrograde ones ($r_{\text{ph}}^+ \to 1.0 r_g$ vs $r_{\text{ph}}^- \to 4.0 r_g$), producing the iconic asymmetric D-shaped shadow.

3. Accretion Disk Physics

The accretion disk is not the black hole itself. It is external glowing plasma orbiting outside the ISCO boundary. Dissipation follows the Novikov-Thorne relativistic gradient with peak emission at the inner edge.

4. Doppler Beaming & Redshift

Relativistic orbital speed causes extreme Doppler boosting ($I_{\text{obs}} = I_{\text{emit}} \cdot g^4$). The approaching disk side is intensely bright and blueshifted, while the receding side is dimmed and redshifted.

5. Gravitational Lensing Arcs

Extreme spacetime curvature deflects light from the rear of the disk over the top and beneath the bottom of the black hole, making both faces of the disk visible simultaneously.

6. Gravitational Time Dilation

Clocks closer to the event horizon tick dramatically slower: $d\tau/dt = \sqrt{1 - 2r/\Sigma}$. Near Miller's planet orbit ($r \approx 1.05 r_g$), 1 hour equals 7 years on Earth.
